ECONOMETRIC ANALYSIS

Three hypotheses. Three verdicts.

GJR-GARCH(1,1,1) volatility model with 7 geopolitical / supply-side controls. Estimated on the thesis observation window: Oct 2023 – Sep 2025, Houthi-era Brent (N = 505 trading days). The regression coefficients shown below are frozen — they are not re-fit against the live HDX feed that powers the Overview tab. Re-running the model on the broader US–Iran war period would conflate two different conflict regimes. To extend the analysis past Sep 2025 a separate war-period regression is needed; that's future work.

HOW TO READ THIS
β (beta) — the size and direction of the effect on conditional volatility (after controls). Positive β means the variable raises volatility; negative β means it lowers it. Bigger absolute value = stronger push.
p-value — the probability of seeing this result by chance alone. Under 0.05 is the usual bar for "statistically significant"; under 0.01 is strong evidence the relationship is real.
R² (r-squared) — fraction of variance the full model explains. Higher means the model captures more of the wiggle in Brent volatility. The remainder is everything else: inventories, OPEC policy, weather, etc.
Verdict logic — SUPPORTED requires p < 0.05 and β in the hypothesised (positive) direction. NOT SUPPORTED · sig. opposite means p < 0.05 but β has the wrong sign — a real effect that contradicts the hypothesis.

§ 05

Attacks vs Volatility · Side-by-Side by Regime

Each point is one week: x = events that week, y = annualized Brent volatility (daily-return std × √252). The two regimes use different units (curated maritime vs. broad political violence), so they're plotted as two separate panels with their own axis scales — much more readable than overlaying them on a shared axis, where the thesis cluster (x ≤ 47) collapses against the war cluster (x up to 250). β is the OLS slope (volatility points added per extra weekly event); R² is fit quality. Flatter line = attacks don't drive vol; steeper = they do.

§ 08

Correlation Matrix

Pairwise Pearson correlations — cyan = positive, crimson = negative. Used to validate variable independence.

How to read: each cell shows how closely two variables move together on a scale from −1 (perfect opposites) to +1 (perfect lockstep). 0 means no linear relationship. Why it matters: if two inputs to the regression are highly correlated (|r| > 0.8), the model can't tell them apart and the individual β estimates become unreliable (multicollinearity). The matrix is how we check that attack frequency, OVX, DXY, and the chokepoint proximity flag are all carrying independent information.

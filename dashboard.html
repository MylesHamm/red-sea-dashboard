ECONOMETRIC ANALYSIS

Three hypotheses. Three verdicts.

GJR-GARCH(1,1,1) volatility model with geopolitical and supply-side controls. Attack frequency explains a small but statistically significant share of Brent volatility variance; chokepoint attacks dominate.

HOW TO READ THIS
β (beta) — the size of the effect. A β of 1.07 means each additional weekly attack is associated with roughly 1.07 percentage points more volatility, holding controls fixed. Bigger β = stronger push.
p-value — the probability this result came from chance alone. Under 0.05 is the usual bar for "real." 0.003 means 3-in-1000 odds of seeing this by luck — very unlikely to be noise.
R² (r-squared) — fraction of variance the model explains. 0.29 means 29% of the wiggle in Brent volatility is accounted for; the rest is everything else (inventories, OPEC, weather). Financial R² is usually low.
Verdict logic — SUPPORTED requires p < 0.05 and β in the expected direction. WEAKLY SUPPORTED means the sign is right but p is between 0.05 and 0.10 (suggestive, not conclusive).

§ 08

Correlation Matrix

Pairwise Pearson correlations — cyan = positive, crimson = negative. Used to validate variable independence.

How to read: each cell shows how closely two variables move together on a scale from −1 (perfect opposites) to +1 (perfect lockstep). 0 means no linear relationship. Why it matters: if two inputs to the regression are highly correlated (|r| > 0.8), the model can't tell them apart and the individual β estimates become unreliable (multicollinearity). The matrix is how we check that attack frequency, OVX, DXY, and the chokepoint proximity flag are all carrying independent information.
